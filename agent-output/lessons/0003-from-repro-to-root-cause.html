The two-sentence root cause

One hole in the server, one enabler in the client. Neither alone produces the mixed dict through the UI. Fill these skeletons — then rewrite them in your own words until you'd say them out loud without notes: 

Server:  The follow-up PATCH on /api/review-student/<uuid>/ only guards
         ____________________; it never asks ____________________, so
         follow-up data meant for a ____________ decision can be merged
         into a proposal that recorded ____________.

Client:  ReviewStudent.jsx treats the server's ______________________
         as ______________, routing the mentor into the ____________
         feedback form, which then supplies the stray PATCH.

Notebook: final versions, own words, no line numbers. If a sentence needs more than two clauses, it's two sentences — that's fine.

Draft the write-up

The take-home deliverable is a short story with four beats. Draft it now, before the fix exists — the fix must live inside your acceptance criterion, not replace it:

  1. What Ops saw — one line, in data terms (their words translated the way you did in lesson 2).
  2. Minimal repro — the click sequence and the equivalent raw requests, ending with the exact final JSON.
  3. Root cause — your two sentences from above.
  4. Acceptance criterion — fill in: "The fix is correct if, for any sequence of ______, the response dict never contains ______." If your blank can't be turned into a test later, sharpen it now.
